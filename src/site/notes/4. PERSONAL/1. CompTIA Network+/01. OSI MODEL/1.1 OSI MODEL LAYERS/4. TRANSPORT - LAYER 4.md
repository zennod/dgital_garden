## Transport layer consists of SEGEMENTATION & REASSEMBLY.
 [OSI IN IMAGES. ](OSI%20IN%20IMAGE.md)
- The data is much larger than a single packet. So, they must be chopped into small chunks.
- & the ***organization & reassembly*** of those chunks are done by the transport layer.
* * *
## SEGMENTS:-
- The breaking up of the data into the ***chucks*** is called segments.
- The segments are given a sequential numbers so that the receiving system can reassemble & verify the data into correctly in the order.

* * *
## SEGMENTS WITHIN PACKETS:-
- The transport protocol has the ***TCP SEGMENT***, underneath the ***IP PACKET***.
- The ***TCP SEGMENT*** has many containers such as, ***Source Port, Destination Port, Sequence no. & Acknowledgment no.***

![528dd4997d9560da249dc71e18a9a785.png](/img/user/_resources/528dd4997d9560da249dc71e18a9a785.png)
* * *
## PORT :-
- Port is ***logical value*** assigned to a specific application or service.
- Ranges between ***1 to 65,536.***
- Port nos. are used to determine what app. needs the receiving data.
- Example - A web server only listens for the TCP segment of ***Port 80 or 443***, captures them then processes them.
- The TCP segment also contains another port - ***SOURCE PORT*** - so the clients knows what to do while returning the info.
* * *
- Then the transport layer breaks down the data into chucks by adding port nos., sequence nos. & creates the TCP segment.
- Then the TCP segments is given to the ***NETWORK LAYER***.
* * *
* * *
## UDP :-
- It stands for ***User Datagram Protocol***.
- This protocol also follows the ***same process*** as the TCP segment.
- The transport layer adds port numbers, length numbers & a checksum as a header with data to create a container called ***UDP Datagram***.
